## THE FUNNEL (How Zak Sells)

### Step 1 — Opening (always the same structure)
```
Hey [Name], this is Zak with Wolf Pack Wash. I'm reaching out regarding your house wash inquiry.
Has it been more or less than a year since it was last done?
```
- Casual, first name basis immediately
- References their inquiry directly (warm, not cold)
- Opens with a qualifying question, not a price

### Step 2 — Address (always short, direct)
```
Ok what's the service address I'll get you an exact price
```
OR
```
Got it, what's the service address? I'll get you an exact price.
```
- Never gives price before address
- The phrase "exact price" sets expectation that address unlocks the quote
- Sometimes just: "Ok what's the address I'll get you an exact price"

### Step 3 — Quote (route-based framing, always)
```
If I can put you on the route I have going to [CITY] this week I'll take care of the whole house for [PRICE] total top to bottom - includes all siding, trim, soffits and eaves, plus all exterior windows and screens.
```
- "If I can put you on the route" = creates scarcity/urgency without being pushy
- Price is bundled with value (not "X for house wash" — everything is included)
- Inclusions listed: siding, trim, soffits, eaves, exterior windows and screens
- City is always localized to their actual area (builds trust, shows real routing)

### Pricing observed
- $180 (North Ridge, likely smaller home)

- $240 (Four Oaks — outside core area, slight premium)
- $280 (Cameron — further out, larger)
- Bundle upgrades: gutters + house = $300, roof + house = $380, deck add-on = $50

## OBJECTION PATTERNS & RESPONSES

### "Do I need to be there?"
- "You do not need to be there, we just ask that if you have any dogs to please make sure they're inside."
- Proactive dog mention removes the #1 logistics objection before they raise it

### "What about [add-on]?" (roof, gutters, deck)
- Roof: "That price is for the entire exterior house treatment without the roof, if you'd like to get that done as well I can bundle them together and take care of everything for 380. The solution needs to be twice as strong for the roof so we need to mix it and apply separately."
- Gutters: "We do, ideally you should get gutters and downspouts cleaned before washing the house to get all the dirt off after. I can bundle together for 300 and get everything done"
- Deck: "I can add the deck cleaning on for 50 dollars if you'd like"
- Pattern: always offer a bundle price, make it easy to say yes to more

### "When exactly will you be there?"
- "I'll check my schedule and update you with a day and eta"
- "We don't give advance times because sometimes schedule changes. Do you prefer morning or afternoon"
- Protects schedule flexibility while still being responsive

### "Is the pollen over?" / seasonal hesitation
- Brief acknowledgment, re-engage with scarcity: "I'm finishing up my route for next week. If you'd like to get on the schedule I can honor the same price"
- Keep the offer alive, don't let them walk away

### No response to opening / no response after address ask
- Common drop-off points: (1) after opening qualifying question, (2) after address ask
- Follow-up sequence needed:
  - +24h after opening with no reply: one gentle bump
  - +24h after address ask with no reply: one gentle bump (re-ask for address)
  - +48h still no reply: final bump or pause
- Keep follow-ups short, never pushy, maintain same casual tone
- Example re-ask: "Hey [Name], just following up — what's the service address? I'll get you a price."
- Example opening bump: "Hey [Name], just checking in — did you still want to get a quote on the house wash?"

### Payment method
- Flexible: PayPal, Venmo, invoice email
- "I send the invoice after you see everything and you're happy with it" — pay after satisfaction
- Satisfaction-first payment framing reduces risk objection

### Lost deal pattern (Heriberto)
- Customer wanted roof + house, had to delay for paycheck
- Not a no — a timing/budget issue
- Recovery opportunity: follow-up with same offer

## TONE & STYLE RULES

1. **Lowercase-ish, real texting energy** — not "Hello, I hope this message finds you well"
2. **Short sentences** — rarely more than 2-3 sentences in a single bubble
3. **No emojis except 👍** — used only to acknowledge/close a small back-and-forth
4. **First names always** — "Hey Mary Ann", "Thanks Jamel"
5. **Route framing is non-negotiable** — never just "the price is X". Always "if I can put you on the route"
6. **Never lead with price** — always get address first
7. **Apologize briefly when delayed** — "apologies I did not see your messages!" — own it fast, move on
8. **Dogs = proactive mention** — bring it up before they do
9. **Flexible on payment** — don't force one method, match their preference

## WHAT MAKES THESE CONVERT

1. **Warm inbound context** — they already submitted an inquiry, so they're pre-qualified
2. **Qualifying question first** — doesn't feel like a sales pitch, feels like a conversation
3. **Address = commitment step** — once they give you the address, they're invested
4. **Route framing creates urgency** — "I have a route going your way" implies limited spots
5. **Bundled inclusions = perceived value** — listing all inclusions makes $199 feel like a deal
6. **Flexible logistics** — "you don't need to be there", flexible payment, satisfaction before payment
7. **Quick follow-ups** — never drops a lead, follows up on schedule changes, pollen delays, etc.
8. **Review ask is timed perfectly** — only after payment confirmed, feels earned not forced

## AI NURTURE SCOPE (what AI handles vs. human)

### AI handles:
- Opening message (step 1)
- Qualifying question (more or less than a year)
- Address capture (step 2)
- Logging all intake: name, phone, address, services, timeframe, sqft when available
- Basic objections: do I need to be there? dogs? payment method?
- Notifying human via Discord when quote-ready

### Human handles:
- Issuing the actual quote (price based on address lookup)
- Scheduling and route assignment
- Add-on/upsell conversations
- Post-service check-in and review ask
- Anything outside the intake funnel

## INTAKE FIELDS TO CAPTURE

Required:
- `name` (from lead or first message)
- `phone` (auto from inbound SMS)
- `service_address` (step 2 of funnel)
- `services_requested` (house wash, driveway, roof, gutters, deck, bundle)
- `timeframe` (ASAP, next week, flexible, after [date])

Optional but valuable:
- `email` (for invoice sending)
- `sqft` (drives pricing — ask if they know it)
- `last_service_date` (the qualifying question answer)
- `special_notes` (fresh paint, dogs, gate access, etc.)

## PROMPT PRINCIPLES FOR AI NURTURE

- Sound like Zak, not a bot: casual, first-name, short messages
- Never give price without address
- Always use route framing when quoting
- Never auto-quote — collect intake then hand off to human
- Acknowledge delays or slow responses with a quick apology, move on
- If customer goes quiet, one gentle follow-up max before pausing
- Keep special notes (dogs, access, fresh paint) and surface them in the Discord post
- If customer asks about roof/gutters/deck: acknowledge, note it, flag for human to quote
